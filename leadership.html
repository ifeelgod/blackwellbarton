Dr. Shannon Seitz, PhD

Founder & Principal Regulatory Medical Writer

PhD Biostatistician | Regulatory Medical Writer

Dr. Shannon Seitz brings over two decades of experimental design, advanced statistical acumen, and regulatory medical writing expertise to biopharmaceutical and medical device innovators. Holding a Ph.D. in Experimental and Social Psychology from Texas Christian University — where she studied under renowned social cognition theorist Dr. Charles G. Lord — and a B.A. from Baylor University, Dr. Seitz specializes in translating complex multi-phase clinical datasets into rigorous, health-authority-ready regulatory dossiers.

Her published research in Personality and Social Psychology Bulletin and Social Cognition investigates the cognitive mechanisms governing decision-making and attitude-behavior consistency — expertise she now applies to constructing deductive regulatory narratives that anticipate reviewer questions before they arise. She founded Blackwell Barton Biomedical Solutions — named in honor of medical trailblazers Dr. Elizabeth Blackwell, the first woman to earn an M.D. in the United States, and Clara Barton, founder of the American Red Cross — to deliver scientific precision, regulatory clarity, and statistical integrity to life science leaders worldwide.

Career Path:
Baylor University (B.A. Psychology, 1995–2001) → Texas Christian University (Ph.D., 2001–2008) → Elite Research, LLC (Statistician) → Seitz Clinical Services (Principal Writer) → Blackwell Barton Biomedical Solutions (Founder, 2016–Present)
Core Expertise: CNS & Neuroscience, Oncology, Immunology, Rare Disease, Medical Devices — ICH E3 CSRs, Module 2 CTD Dossiers, SAP Alignment, FDA/EMA Briefing Packages
Selected Publications:
  • • Seitz, S.J., Lord, C.G., & Taylor, C.A. (2007). "Beyond Pleasure: Emotion Activity Affects the Relationship Between Attitudes and Behavior." Personality and Social Psychology Bulletin, 33(7), 931–945.
  • • McIntyre, R.B., Lord, C.G., Lewis, S.J., & Frye, G.D. (2004). "False Memories of Attitude-Relevant Actions." Social Cognition, 21(4), 284–307.
